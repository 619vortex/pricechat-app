### v2, qwen3.8:27b p2 "not-major"

- F578 (now a MISS);
- F929: it now reads the top-10 threshold as 15% (**threshold confusion**);
- N2: lead-clause framing.

### The causes, ranked

1. **Under-tiering of strikes on NATO or Gulf soil and of mega-cap moves.** The "established war" discount, and "no casualties = not major".
2. **Attribution read as speculation** ("…, says Tusk").
3. **Consequence kind and event type mislabelled.**
   - Missiles have been labelled `money_amount`.
   - Poland has been labelled `macro_data`.
   - This also weakens any trigger built on those fields.
4. **Missing context.**
   - Headline-only items lose the deal size, as in T45684.
   - Overall, though, headline-only items score no worse: newsworthy accuracy is 0.88–0.89 on headline-only items, against 0.85–0.88 overall.
5. **Hedge misfire on "set to".** gemma only; qwen gets it right.
6. **Label disagreements worth the owner's review.**
   - Commodity moves of 4–5% and the "global" Microsoft outage are S in the labels. The prompt's own ladder ("index 4%+", "global outage") would make them major.
   - Most "major false positives" on dev are these.

## 3. Prompt p2 (`intel-item-v1/p2` in `backend/app/intel/prompt.py`)

**What it adds** (written from dev-split failures only; the schema is unchanged):
- Attribution is not speculation.
- A trailing-clause hedge does not hedge the lead.
- An explicit list of majors with no figure (missiles on NATO soil, a strike on a Gulf capital or state assets, NATO troops in a war, a direct state-on-state exchange, a mega-cap or new $100B+ listing moving 25%+, a $10B+ deal, a correction or bear market).
- A consequence-kind glossary.
- Round-up and reaction pieces are features.
- Routine flows are notable at most.

**Reference rows on test:**

| Engine | Prec | Rec | F1 | Major publish recall | Major-as-major recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rules | 0.53 | 0.32 | 0.40 | 0.79 | 0.21 |

**Reading the test split.** p2 clearly helps breaking recall: majors flagged as major rise from 10 to 12 of 14, and overall recall rises by 0.04. But:
- major-as-major precision falls from 0.43 to 0.32 (26 false "major" calls, against 13);
- editor recall falls from 0.87 to 0.80 (on 18 editor-labelled test items).

So it is **not a clear win**, and the default stays p1. **Recommendation:** use p2 for the breaking/major lane only behind an adjudicator (Sonnet/Opus, once measured), or tighten its major list in a p3 against a fresh labelled set.

## 4. gemma4:31b as a second opinion (`backend/app/intel/second_opinion.py`)

**Setup**
- Primary: qwen3.8:27b. Second: gemma4:31b with the p1 prompt; gemma was run on all 813 items.
- Policies are scored offline by `ops/intel/score.py --second`.
- Latency is gemma's p50/p95 on the escalated items only. The cost of loading the second model is extra (see section 5).

**Escalation triggers**
- `sig_hard_large`: qwen says significant (not opinion), with a hard consequence kind of large size. Large means 4%+, $10B+, 10+ casualties, or an escalation or disruption with no figure.
- `sig_hard_event`: significant with a conflict, market, hack, outage, shipping or M&A event type.
- `flag_suppressed`: major or significant, but killed by the speculative or opinion flag.
- `major_hedged`: major resting on a hedged claim.
- `primary_major`: confirm every major.
- `rules_disagree`: rules newsworthy or major while qwen is not.

**Combine modes**
- **promote**: the second opinion can only raise.
- **both**: major only if both models say major.
- **arbiter**: gemma decides major.
- **replace**: gemma's decision replaces qwen's.

### What the second opinion does and does not do

**What sig_hard_large / promote changed on test:**
- Two majors promoted: F929 "NVIDIA TUMBLES 14%" and N2 "KOSPI halted after 8% crash".
- Three non-majors promoted:
  - F882 "Bitcoin sets new selloff low" (S);
  - T45328, a $48B SBF "windfall" feature (X);
  - N359, the Microsoft outage live page (X).

**On dev,** it promoted 12 non-majors and no majors:
- gold and oil moves of 4–5%;
- Microsoft global-outage items;
- Dell's $25B buyback.

Several of these are **label-boundary cases** (see section 2, cause 6) rather than clear errors.

**Major-precision check.** `major_check/both` halves the false "major" calls, from 26 to 13 on test. But it loses 4 of 12 real majors (0.86 to 0.57). gemma itself under-tiers Gulf and Crimea strikes, so it cannot be the precision check.

### Recommendation

**Policy `sig_hard_large` + `promote`.** It is the `DEFAULT_POLICY` and `DEFAULT_COMBINE` in `second_opinion.py`.
- On test, major-as-major recall rises from 0.86 to 1.00. Publish recall and F1 are unchanged.
- About 13% of items are escalated (15% on dev).
- Added latency on escalated items: gemma p50 8.2 s, p95 12.4 s.
- It costs 3 extra false "major" calls on test, and 12 on dev.

The evidence is thin: +2 majors on 14, and none on dev's 28. So:
- run it in **SHADOW** first (record `escalation_reasons` and the combined decision next to the verdict);
- leave major **precision** to the planned cloud adjudicator, not to gemma.

Rejected:
- `major_check` / `both` / `arbiter`: they lose too many real majors.
- Wider triggers: 2–4× the escalations for no extra recall.
- gemma as the primary: F1 0.63 against 0.74, and 2.7× slower.

### How the worker would call it (not implemented)

1. After the qwen `intel.analyze` job, the worker calls `should_escalate(analysis, gate.rules_view(...))`.
2. When it returns True, the worker runs the same prompt on gemma4:31b, batched per poll to pay one model swap.
3. It returns both analyses.
4. Railway computes `combine(primary, second)` and stores the reasons.
5. A gemma failure or timeout means `combine(primary, None)`, which is the primary's own decision; nothing blocks.

## 5. Wall clock and GPU

**Machine and load**
- RTX 5090 (32 GB), Ollama, one model resident at a time.
- World of Warcraft was running during every run: about 6.5 GB of VRAM used before Ollama loaded, and the GPU already partly busy.

**Run times**

| Run | Wall clock |
|---|---|
| qwen p1, 598 new items | ≈ 36 min |
| qwen p2, dev 565 items | ≈ 30 min |
| qwen p2, test 248 items | ≈ 13 min |
| gemma p1, 598 new items | ≈ 1 h 45 min |

Phase 0's v1 runs were reused.

**Latency per item**
- qwen: p50 3.0–3.5 s, p95 3.9–4.4 s.
- gemma: p50 8.0 s, p95 14.7 s.

**Swap cost.** A qwen↔gemma swap costs about 8–10 s of load, so the two 27–31B q4 models do not both stay resident in 32 GB next to other GPU load. Escalations should therefore be batched.

**Cost:** $0 (no paid calls).
